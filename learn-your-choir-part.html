How to learn your choir part

Rehearsals go faster when everyone arrives knowing their notes. But most choir music is printed as a full score, and picking out your own line, especially as an alto or tenor in the middle of the harmony, is hard if you don't read music fluently. Partsong reads the score for you and plays just your part.

1. Get the music into Partsong

Photograph each page with your phone, or open the PDF your choir sent. For photos, hold the phone square to the page in good light, one page per photo. Partsong reads the music on your device; nothing is uploaded.

2. Check the lines of music (systems)

Before it reads the notes, Partsong shows each page with red lines where it thinks one line of music ends and the next begins. A line of music (a system) is all the staves sung together, from the left margin to the right. Partsong needs these right to know which staff belongs to which voice.

If a red line is in the wrong place, tap between two staves to add or remove one. Pages that look unusual are flagged so you know where to look. When the lines look right, tap Next and Partsong reads the music.

3. Choose your part, or mark it on the music

Pick soprano, alto, tenor or bass from the list, or whichever line is yours. If the music divides (two sopranos, a solo), each line is listed separately.

Some scores change layout from page to page: a hymn with the verses on two staves and the last verse on four, or a voice that moves to another staff. Then tap Mark your part on the music and tap the staff you sing in each system. Where two voices share a staff (soprano and alto on one, say), tap once for the upper voice and again for the lower; a third tap unmarks it. Marking works for any score, and Partsong plays exactly what you marked.

4. Listen to your line on its own

Play just your part, with the score following along so you see where you are. Take it a few bars at a time: listen, then sing it back.

5. Sing along, then add the others

Once the notes are familiar, sing with your line. Then bring in the other parts, so you learn to hold yours against the harmony, which is what you'll need in rehearsal.

6. Take it with you

Send yourself a recording of your part to practise in the car or on a walk, or send the score to someone else in your section.

Tips for each voice

  • Sopranos usually have the tune. The work is in the high passages and the breathing: mark where you'll breathe.
  • Altos often sit on a few repeated notes under the tune. Listen for which note of the chord you're on; that's what pulls you off it.
  • Tenors usually read a treble clef (often with a small 8 under it) and sing an octave lower; in hymn books they share the bass clef. Listen for your line inside the chord, as it's easy to drift to the tune.
  • Basses carry the harmony. Learn the big leaps first; the rest follows.

Does it work with my music?

Partsong reads printed choral music: hymns, anthems, carols, part-songs, from a photo or a PDF. It works best on clean, straight pages. Handwritten music isn't supported. It runs in the browser on Android phones and on computers; on iPhones and iPads reading is less reliable, but scores shared from Partsong open there.
